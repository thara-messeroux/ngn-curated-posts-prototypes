NUEAC-398 · Phase 1 · Concepts

Three ways to fix the Curated Posts screen

Curated Posts is the screen that controls every automatically-filled shelf on Northeastern Global News — the homepage rails, the Most read lists, the section fronts. There are 47 shelves on it. All of them render open, in the order they were created, with no way to search. These are three working prototypes of the same data, built on WordPress's own admin design system so any of them drops in without a restyle.

What the screen costs today

47shelves on one page
~40screens of scroll at 1440px
0ways to search or filter
~376label/value lines rendered on load

Three problems, worst first

1 · Finding one shelf means reading all of them

No search, no filter, no jump list. Reaching "Most read health" means scrolling past 29 other blocks and reading 13px titles until you spot it.

2 · Every shelf is expanded, including its empty fields

Each block prints all eight settings whether or not they hold a value. Roughly half of what is on screen reads None, N/A or Off.

3 · Delete sits beside Rename and Duplicate

Same size, same row, one gap apart, on every card. Nothing warns that a shelf is live on the site before it is removed.

The three directions

Direction A

List table

The screen becomes a standard WordPress list table — the same component the Posts screen uses. Sortable columns, search where editors already look, row actions on hover.

Best for
Editors who live in wp-admin already
Strength
Zero learning curve, least code to write
Trade-off
One row per shelf means less room for the rules
Open direction A
Direction B

Grouped accordion

The 47 shelves fall into six families that already exist in their names. Each family collapses. The toolbar sticks to the top of the screen so search stays reachable however far down you are.

Best for
Working through one family at a time
Strength
Matches how the shelves are actually maintained
Trade-off
One more click to reach a single shelf
Open direction B
Direction C

Two-pane workbench

A searchable index on the left that never moves, and the selected shelf's settings on the right. Changing shelf swaps only the right pane, so your place in the list is never lost. Each slot shows the headline that is live on the site right now.

Best for
Editing six shelves in a row
Strength
The only direction that shows what a shelf actually holds
Trade-off
Least like the rest of wp-admin, most to build
Open direction C

Query settings — three designs

The dialog behind the icon on every shelf is where the real work happens, and it is the part the redesign had not touched. It asks for sixteen post types, nine taxonomies under each, and hundreds of terms under those, in one 510px column with no summary and no feedback. These three rebuild it on NGN's own v2 design system, whose tokens are Material 3 named and whose primary colour is Northeastern red.

Settings A

Guided panel

Four numbered steps in one column. A plain-English sentence and a live match count sit above them, so you always know what the rules mean and how many stories they catch.

Settings E · recommended

The sentence, in the pane

C's rule written as one plain English line you could read aloud to anyone, moved out of the dialog and into D's pane. Click any underlined word to change it. The shelves stay on screen, unsaved work is kept per shelf, and the live count sits under the sentence so you can see what the rule actually returns.

Open E
Recommendation: E. The sentence from C, the pane from D.

A scaled and showed the result but sat over the list. B never covered the list but had no room. C read beautifully and broke on long rules because a dialog is only so wide. D fixed the width but went back to labelled fields. E is C's sentence in D's pane: the rule still reads as English, and the pane gives it the room the dialog never had. Checked against all four tests — two columns at 1280 and 1440 with no horizontal scroll, axe finds no WCAG 2.1 AA violations, the live count updates on every keystroke, and switching shelf mid-edit keeps the unsaved work and marks it in the list.

Recommendation: C, with A as the fallback.

All three solve the three problems. Only C answers the question editors actually have — what is on this shelf right now? — by showing the live headline in each slot instead of a number. That is worth the extra build. If the build cost comes back too high, Direction A plus B's grouping as view links gets most of the value for a fraction of the work, and the comparison is already here to make that call with.
